# Prism v2.2 end goal

## The product at the end of v2.2

Prism is no longer one general assistant trying to handle every responsibility. It is a private
workspace where you create several specialized **Prisms**, give each one a clear job, and return to
its own conversation whenever you want an update or action.

Each Prism understands:

- What its job is.
- What information matters to that job.
- Which Teams chats it may monitor.
- When to run.
- What deserves an immediate alert.
- How you want its updates written.
- What it may recommend.
- Which actions always require your approval.

The Prisms share your Microsoft connection and model provider, but they do not share learned
memory, instructions, or conversations.

## Creating your first Prism

You open the **Prisms** page and select **Create Prism**. Prism offers:

- **Executive Assistant** — the default guided template.
- **Blank Prism** — a role defined entirely by you.

You choose Executive Assistant, name it **Executive Assistant Prism**, and write:

> Keep me updated about important things happening across my Microsoft Teams throughout the day.
> Be friendly and concise. Tell me immediately about serious risks, and give me a daily evening
> summary. When follow-up is needed, prepare the message for my approval.

The new Prism begins a short onboarding conversation. Its questions adapt to your description:

> Which situations should interrupt you immediately?

You select production incidents, serious blockers, missed deadlines, important unanswered
questions, and customer escalations.

> When should I prepare your regular brief?

You choose weekdays at 6:00 PM in Asia/Kolkata.

> Should I monitor every accessible Teams chat?

You keep the default, **All chats**, and exclude one social group.

> What should a useful update contain?

You choose a short summary, why it matters, owner, deadline, evidence, and suggested next step.

The Prism then shows its working agreement:

```text
Mission
Keep Yash informed about important work across Microsoft Teams.

Immediate alerts
Production incidents, serious blockers, missed deadlines,
important unanswered questions, and customer escalations.

Regular brief
Weekdays at 6:00 PM, Asia/Kolkata.

Sources
All accessible Teams chats except Social Group.

Style
Friendly, concise, direct, and evidence-backed.

Actions
May prepare Teams-message drafts. Every message requires approval.
```

You confirm it. Only then does the Prism become active.

## A normal working day

### 10:12 AM — a proactive incident update

While the Prism app is open, several engineers discuss a failed production deployment in Teams.
The Executive Assistant Prism incrementally reads the new activity and finds:

- A migration failed.
- Rollback is being discussed.
- No clear owner has accepted responsibility.
- No decision deadline is visible.

It stores the supporting messages, checks that the issue matches its working agreement, and adds an
update to its conversation. A browser notification appears:

> **Executive Assistant: Production deployment may be blocked**

Opening it takes you directly to the update:

> **Production deployment may be blocked — High confidence**
>
> The database migration failed at 10:06 AM. The team is discussing rollback, but I could not find
> a confirmed owner or decision deadline.
>
> **Why this matters:** the release is currently at risk and the next action is unclear.
>
> **Suggested next step:** ask Rohan to confirm the rollback owner and provide a go/no-go decision
> by 10:30 AM.

You can expand the exact Teams evidence. The update clearly separates verified facts from Prism's
recommendation.

Because a follow-up is useful, it also prepares an approval card:

> Rohan, please confirm who owns the rollback and share the go/no-go decision by 10:30 AM. Please
> also post the current customer impact, if any.

Nothing has been sent. You edit the time to 10:45 AM and approve. The existing v2.1 action engine
sends exactly that revision through your Microsoft Teams account and records the result.

### 1:00 PM — you ask a question

Inside the same Executive Assistant Prism conversation, you ask:

> What changed after the deployment alert?

The Prism reads the relevant recent evidence and its own incident context. It reports that the
rollback completed, names the owner, and identifies one unresolved monitoring issue. This manual
request does not create a draft unless you ask for one or the Prism offers a clearly optional
recommendation.

### 6:00 PM — scheduled brief

The scheduled run adds an evening brief to the Executive Assistant Prism:

```text
Today at a glance
- Production rollback completed at 10:38 AM.
- Search release moved to Thursday; QA acknowledged the new date.
- Two customer questions remain unanswered for more than four hours.

Needs your attention tomorrow
- Confirm the owner for the monitoring follow-up.
- Decide whether the customer questions need an executive response.

What I could not verify
- No final post-incident review time was posted in the chats I could access.
```

It does not repeat the morning incident as a new alert. It links back to the earlier incident and
includes only the material change.

## Creating another specialized Prism

You create a blank Prism named **QA Release Prism** and describe it:

> Watch release and testing discussions. Track reported defects, regressions, blocked test runs,
> missing reproduction steps, owners, and expected fix dates. Give me a QA readiness update every
> weekday afternoon.

Its onboarding questions differ from the Executive Assistant's. It asks about release scope,
severity levels, readiness criteria, relevant chats, update time, and desired report structure.
Its final working agreement and memory belong only to the QA Release Prism.

The QA Prism may see the same production message as the Executive Assistant, but it interprets it
through a testing role. The underlying event is deduplicated, so you do not receive two identical
browser alerts. The QA Prism can still record a separate QA-specific observation in its own
conversation if it has something materially different to add.

## Where you spend your time

The main **Prisms** page is a compact directory:

```text
Executive Assistant     Active     1 urgent · 2 unread
QA Release Prism        Active     Next run 4:30 PM
Customer Watch Prism    Paused     Monitoring off
```

There is no second Teams inbox and no combined stream of every update. You spend your time:

- Opening the Prism whose responsibility you care about.
- Reading short proactive updates instead of every Teams message.
- Inspecting evidence when a decision matters.
- Correcting the Prism's understanding when necessary.
- Reviewing and approving exact proposed actions.
- Adjusting the working agreement when the job changes.

Teams remains the place where people communicate. Prism is the place where your specialized
assistants understand, prioritize, recommend, and prepare safe actions.

## What v2.2 reliably does

By the end of v2.2, the product can:

- Create multiple private Prisms from a blank setup or Executive Assistant template.
- Generate safe, dynamic onboarding questions from a role description.
- Support selectable choices plus custom answers.
- Produce and version a user-confirmed working agreement.
- Keep a separate conversation and memory boundary for every Prism.
- Monitor all accessible Teams chats by default, with optional inclusions and exclusions.
- Run manually, on a schedule, from detected events, and through continuous incremental checks.
- Work locally without requiring a public webhook by using bounded polling.
- Identify production incidents, deadline risks, blockers, unanswered messages, and important
  customer discussions when those signals belong to a Prism's job.
- Publish evidence-backed proactive updates inside the correct Prism conversation.
- Show browser notifications while Prism is open and permission is granted.
- Deduplicate repeated events and alerts conservatively.
- Recommend next steps and prepare approval requests.
- Reuse v2.1 to edit, approve, send, and audit exact Teams messages.
- Pause and resume monitoring without losing history or cursors.
- Recover safely after an API restart.

## What v2.2 deliberately does not do

v2.2 does not:

- Act autonomously outside Prism.
- Send a Teams message without approval.
- Run while the local API is stopped.
- Deliver browser notifications while the Prism page is closed.
- Guarantee continuous cloud monitoring.
- Combine every Prism's updates into one central inbox.
- Share memory between Prisms.
- Allow multiple users or shared Prisms.
- Monitor Teams channels, Outlook, calendars, Jira, Slack, or ClickUp.
- Put the Prism assistant inside Microsoft Teams.

## The end-state statement

Prism v2.2 is complete when this is true:

> “I can create several private AI assistants, explain each job in plain language, confirm how each
> one should work, and trust them to monitor my Teams activity and proactively brief me in their
> own web conversations. Their memories stay separate, repeated alerts are controlled, and any
> Teams message they prepare is sent only after I review and approve the exact content.”
