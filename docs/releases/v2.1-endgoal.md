Prism v2.0 is the overall manager-agent vision. Prism v2.1 is the first working product: a
context-aware web-based manager agent that gives you one continuing conversation for understanding
and coordinating work across authorized Teams conversations. It can reason, draft, show its work,
request approval, send, and learn.

It will not yet be a fully autonomous manager. Its job is to remove the repetitive “catch up, understand, compose, send” loop.

## The Prism v2.1 experience

```text
Manager asks Prism a question in the web app
          ↓
Prism creates a job and shows visible progress
          ↓
Resolves the relevant people and Teams conversations
          ↓
Reads a bounded, disclosed set of sources and retrieves work context
          ↓
Identifies what needs attention
          ↓
Drafts one or more coordinated responses with evidence
          ↓
Prism presents one approval card per proposed action
          ↓
Manager changes, approves or rejects each action
          ↓
Prism sends only the exact approved actions
          ↓
Result is recorded and preferences are learned
```

Your main place in Prism v2.1 is one **Prism conversation** in the web app. You tell Prism the
outcome you want, inspect which sources it used, follow progress, and review inline approval cards.
Teams people and groups do not become separate Prism chats. A source filter is available only when
you want to constrain a request.

You use Prism’s web app for agent interaction and Microsoft Teams for human conversations.
Employees remain entirely in Teams and do not need to install or interact with Prism. Approved
messages are sent through Microsoft Graph from your connected Teams account.

## A day in your role

Imagine you manage ten people:

- Five software engineers
- Three AI/ML engineers
- One QA engineer
- One DevOps engineer

The team is preparing a release of an AI-powered search feature.

### 9:15 AM — You return from another meeting

A Teams conversation contains 35 new messages. Developers discussed an API change, the AI team changed the expected response format, QA found failures, and someone asked you to decide whether to delay testing.

Today, you would read everything, reconstruct the sequence and write a response.

With Prism v2.1, you open your continuing Prism conversation and write:

> What needs my attention across the release team this morning?

Prism updates the job progress in the same web conversation:

```text
Job: Review release-team activity

✓ Resolving relevant people and conversations
✓ Reading 47 messages across 3 disclosed Teams chats
✓ Identifying participants
✓ Matching known projects and tasks
✓ Looking for questions and unresolved decisions
✓ Preparing recommendation
```

After processing, Prism answers:

> Three items need your attention:
>
> 1. Arjun changed the API response schema at 11:20 AM.
> 2. Meera says the frontend integration is blocked until the new schema is documented.
> 3. Rohan asked whether QA should delay today’s regression run.
>
> Suggested decision: ask Arjun to publish the schema by 12:30 PM and let QA continue testing unaffected flows.

Each statement links back to the Teams message that supports it.

### 9:18 AM — Prism prepares coordinated actions

Prism presents separate approval cards in the same conversation. For example, one group update and
one private follow-up:

> **Proposed Teams reply**
>
> Arjun, please publish the updated response schema by 12:30 PM and tag Meera when it is ready. Meera, continue with the unaffected frontend work for now. Rohan, please proceed with regression testing on the existing flows and hold only the AI search scenarios until the schema is available.
>
> **Recipients:** AI search conversation  
> **Reason:** Resolve the API dependency without stopping unrelated QA work.  
> **Status:** Waiting for approval

You can:

- Edit the draft in the card
- Approve and send from the card
- Reject it from the card
- Ask Prism for another version in Agent Chat
- Ask why it made the recommendation
- Approve one action while rejecting another

Suppose you change “12:30 PM” to “1:00 PM” and select **Approve and send**.

Prism sends exactly that version—nothing else.

### 9:20 AM — The work is recorded

The web job timeline becomes:

```text
✓ Conversation reviewed
✓ Three attention items found
✓ Reply drafted
✓ Manager edited the deadline
✓ Manager approved
✓ Teams message delivered
```

Prism records that:

- You prefer assigning a clear owner.
- You prefer including a specific deadline.
- You avoid stopping unrelated work when one component is blocked.
- You edited the suggested deadline before approving.

It will use these preferences when preparing future drafts, but they do not grant permission to send messages automatically.

## Later that day

Meera sends you a private message:

> Arjun shared the schema, but one field is still unclear. Should I wait or implement it as optional?

In the same Prism conversation, you ask:

> Read Meera’s latest message and draft a reply.

Prism combines:

- Meera’s message
- The earlier group discussion
- The approved decision
- The known project context
- Your communication preferences

It proposes:

> Please implement the field as optional for now and document the assumption in the PR. Tag Arjun for confirmation before merging.

You approve the web card, and Prism sends the reply through Graph as you.

The important difference is that you did not need to reconstruct the earlier discussion again.

## Where you spend your time

Inside Prism, most of your time will be spent on decisions—not operating the software.

A typical interaction should look like:

- 30–60 seconds explaining what outcome you want
- 1–3 minutes reviewing Prism’s evidence and proposed response
- A few seconds editing or approving
- No time manually copying Teams messages into Prism
- No time manually reconstructing the same context repeatedly

Approvals appear inline in the same Prism conversation:

```text
Needs your approval
├── Reply to Meera about API schema
├── Response to QA about regression testing
└── Clarification requested by DevOps
```

The same conversation is where you delegate work:

- “What needs my response?”
- “Draft a concise reply.”
- “Why is Meera blocked?”
- “Make this less formal.”
- “Mention the deadline.”
- “Show me the messages supporting this.”
- “Send the approved version.”

## What Prism v2.1 does well

By the end of v2.1, Prism should reliably:

- Hold a natural conversation with you in the Prism web app.
- Resolve and read relevant authorized Teams conversations on demand.
- Let you optionally constrain a request to one person or chat.
- Synthesize work across multiple chats with message-level evidence.
- Understand participants and relevant work context.
- Identify questions, blockers and decisions.
- Remember useful manager preferences.
- Draft one or more context-aware, coordinated responses.
- Explain what evidence it used.
- Show progress while working.
- Present web approval cards and let you edit, approve or reject every action independently.
- Send only exact approved messages.
- Record the result for future context.
- Avoid duplicate sends if something is retried.

## What Prism v2.1 does not do yet

To keep this release focused, it will not yet:

- Monitor every team conversation automatically.
- Read Teams channels.
- Read or answer Outlook email.
- Schedule meetings.
- Run unattended multi-hour coordination workflows.
- Execute autonomous actions.
- Operate as a private Prism bot inside Microsoft Teams.
- Join or participate directly in employee group chats and channels as a bot.
- Use Jira, ClickUp or Slack.

Those belong to later Prism v2.x releases.

## The end goal of Prism v2.1

The release is successful when this statement becomes true:

> “I can talk to one Prism manager agent, ask what matters across my team, inspect the Teams
> evidence it used, approve coordinated actions individually, and have Prism safely send the exact
> approved messages from my account—without navigating a second copy of my Teams inbox.”

Prism v2.1 is therefore not yet your replacement manager. It is your trusted web-based
communication and decision copilot: it works across relevant Teams threads, remembers the context,
prepares coordinated actions, and leaves every judgment with you. A later Prism v2.x release can move this manager
experience into Microsoft Teams while keeping the same backend architecture.
