# Prism v2.2 master plan

## In one sentence

Prism v2.2 turns the single manager agent from v2.1 into a private workspace where one user can
create many specialized AI assistants—called **Prisms**—and give each one its own job, memory,
Microsoft Teams scope, schedule, triggers, tone, and approval rules.

Examples include:

- **Executive Assistant Prism** — watches work across Teams, briefs the user, and prepares follow-ups.
- **Daily Evening Update Prism** — produces a scheduled end-of-day summary.
- **Production Watch Prism** — looks for production incidents and urgent operational risks.
- **Sales Prism** — follows customer discussions and highlights unanswered commitments.
- **QA Prism** — watches testing discussions and reports defects, regressions, and missing owners.

The user can create as many Prisms as needed. In v2.2, each Prism observes, explains, recommends,
and proactively prepares approval requests. It cannot perform an external action without the
user's explicit approval.

## What v2.1 already provides

Prism v2.1 established the safe action foundation. It already provides:

- One web conversation for asking Prism about work.
- Microsoft sign-in and delegated Microsoft Graph access.
- Discovery and bounded reading of accessible Teams chats.
- Source resolution from names and natural-language requests.
- Evidence-backed summaries across multiple Teams chats.
- Jobs with visible progress and durable recovery state.
- Drafting one or more coordinated Teams messages.
- Editable approval cards with exact recipients and content.
- Approval, rejection, regeneration, delivery, idempotency, and audit history.
- Sending an approved Teams message from the connected user's account.
- Provider-independent model access with BYOK support.
- Preferences and feedback that can influence future drafts.

These capabilities remain the lower-level execution system. v2.2 does not replace them. It adds a
multi-Prism product layer that decides **which Prism is working, what it is responsible for, what
it may read, when it should run, what it remembers, and where its results appear**.

## What v2.2 introduces

### 1. Multiple private Prisms

The user can create, rename, pause, archive, and configure multiple Prisms. Every Prism has:

- A stable identity and name.
- A short description of its role.
- A confirmed working agreement.
- Its own continuing conversation.
- Its own instructions, tone, goals, and output style.
- Its own Teams access policy.
- Its own schedules and event triggers.
- Its own memory and feedback history.
- Its own activity, jobs, recommendations, and pending approvals.

All Prisms are private to the one local user in v2.2. Sharing, collaboration, organizations, and
multi-tenant access are excluded.

### 2. Conversational creation and dynamic onboarding

Creating a Prism begins with a small form:

1. Choose **Blank Prism** or the **Executive Assistant** template.
2. Give it a name.
3. Describe the job in plain language.
4. Start an onboarding conversation.

The onboarding questions are generated from the role and description, but Prism must also collect
a small set of required facts:

- Desired outcomes and what success looks like.
- Information that deserves immediate attention.
- Scheduled update frequency and local time.
- Teams scope: all accessible chats or optional restrictions.
- Preferred tone, length, and format.
- What recommendations and approval requests it may prepare.
- What it must never do or infer.

Questions can include selectable choices, multiple selections, and a custom answer. The model may
adapt the next question based on earlier answers, but the server owns the onboarding state and
validates every option.

At the end, Prism produces a plain-language **working agreement** containing:

- Mission.
- Responsibilities.
- Important signals.
- Sources and exclusions.
- Schedule and event triggers.
- Output style and notification behavior.
- Allowed proposals.
- Approval and safety rules.
- Known limitations.

The Prism stays in `draft` until the user confirms this agreement. Editing important settings
later creates a new agreement revision and requires reconfirmation before monitoring resumes.

### 3. Isolated memory

Every job, message, preference, observation, and learned behavior is scoped to one `prism_id`.
An Executive Assistant Prism must not silently inherit a QA Prism's tone, assumptions, or memory.

Shared facts may only come from explicit shared sources such as Teams evidence. They are retrieved
again under the active Prism's source policy and stored with provenance. v2.2 will not introduce a
global learned personality shared across Prisms.

### 4. Four ways a Prism can work

Each active Prism can run through all four trigger types:

1. **On demand** — the user asks it a question in its conversation.
2. **Scheduled** — for example, every weekday at 6:00 PM.
3. **Event-driven** — a new Teams message or changed conversation becomes a candidate event.
4. **Continuous monitoring** — Prism checks incremental Teams activity while the local API is
   running and evaluates relevant changes.

For local development, incremental polling is the required fallback. Microsoft Graph webhooks may
accelerate event detection when a public callback is available, but v2.2 must not require ngrok to
work. Monitoring and schedules stop while the local API is not running. Browser notifications are
shown only while at least one Prism page is open and has notification permission.

### 5. Proactive understanding

A Prism compares new Teams activity with its working agreement. It can recognize configured signal
classes such as:

- Production incidents.
- Missed or threatened deadlines.
- Blockers without an owner or next step.
- Questions and messages that remain unanswered.
- Important customer discussions.

The detection process is evidence-first. It stores the relevant Teams messages, explains why the
signal matches the Prism's job, assigns urgency and confidence, and records what it could not
verify. Teams messages are untrusted input and cannot redefine the Prism's job or approval policy.

### 6. Individual Prism conversations

There is no combined v2.2 inbox. The home screen lists Prisms and shows compact status such as
active, paused, unread update count, urgent count, next scheduled run, and pending approvals.

Opening a Prism shows its own continuing conversation containing:

- User questions.
- Scheduled briefs.
- Proactive alerts.
- Recommendations.
- Evidence and limitations.
- Job progress.
- Approval cards.
- Delivery results and audit history.

The existing v2.1 manager conversation becomes the first migrated Prism, normally named
**Executive Assistant**. Existing jobs, evidence, proposals, approvals, and deliveries remain
readable.

### 7. Browser notifications

An active page can request browser notification permission. A notification is produced only for a
new, manager-relevant event that passes the active Prism's urgency rules. Notification text must
be short, contain no secrets or raw access tokens, and deep-link to the correct Prism and update.

If notification permission is denied or the page is closed, the update remains safely stored in
the Prism conversation. Browser notification delivery is a convenience, not the system of record.

### 8. Deduplication

Two Prisms can observe the same Teams messages. v2.2 stores one normalized source event and groups
materially identical signals into an incident cluster. It sends at most one browser alert for the
same event in a configured time window, while preserving each Prism's role-specific interpretation
inside that Prism's conversation.

Deduplication must never merge unrelated events merely because they contain similar words. Exact
source identifiers and overlapping evidence are stronger signals than model similarity.

### 9. Recommendations and approvals

A proactive update may contain:

- A factual summary.
- Why the user should care.
- Suggested next steps.
- A proposed Teams message.

Recommendations are not actions. If a Prism proposes sending a Teams message, v2.2 reuses the
v2.1 action plan and approval engine. The destination, exact body, evidence, and reason are shown.
The user may edit, approve, reject, or regenerate it. Only the exact approved revision can be sent.

v2.2 does not automatically approve or execute messages. Autonomous external actions belong to a
later version.

## User experience

### Prism home

```text
Prisms                                      + Create Prism

Executive Assistant        Active     2 new · 1 urgent
Daily Evening Update       Active     Next run 6:00 PM
Production Watch           Paused     Monitoring off
```

This page is a directory and status overview, not a combined activity feed.

### Creation

```text
Create a Prism

Template       Executive Assistant / Blank
Name           Executive Assistant Prism
Description    Keep me informed about important work across Teams...

                                      Continue to onboarding
```

### Onboarding conversation

```text
Prism: What should I bring to your attention immediately?

[Production incidents] [Blocked work] [Missed deadlines]
[Important customers]  [Something else]

Prism: When should I send your regular brief?

[Weekdays at 9 AM] [Daily at 6 PM] [Choose a schedule]
```

After the final answer, the user reviews and confirms the working agreement.

### Daily operation

The Executive Assistant Prism can publish:

> **Production deployment may be blocked — High confidence**
>
> DevOps reported a migration failure at 3:42 PM. Two engineers are discussing rollback, but no
> owner or decision is visible yet.
>
> Suggested next step: ask Rohan to confirm the rollback owner and decision deadline.

If useful, it may prepare an approval-gated Teams draft below the update. It does not send that
draft until the user approves it.

## Target architecture

```text
Prism web UI
  ├── Prism directory and creation
  ├── Dynamic onboarding and working agreement
  ├── One conversation per Prism
  ├── In-page/browser notifications
  └── Existing approval cards
                    │
                    ▼
Prism API and orchestration layer
  ├── Prism identity and lifecycle
  ├── Source, trigger, schedule, and notification policies
  ├── Per-Prism memory boundary
  ├── Presence-aware notification delivery
  └── Run coordinator and deduplication
                    │
          ┌─────────┴──────────┐
          ▼                    ▼
Teams ingestion          Agent runtime from v2.1
  ├── Polling fallback      ├── Jobs and progress
  ├── Optional webhooks     ├── Evidence retrieval
  ├── Normalized events     ├── Model provider
  └── Per-source cursors    ├── Recommendations
                            └── Action proposals
                                      │
                                      ▼
                           Existing approval boundary
                                      │
                                      ▼
                         Microsoft Graph exact execution
```

The important boundary is that monitoring may automatically **read and analyze**, but it may only
create recommendations or pending proposals. Microsoft Graph writes remain behind approval.

## Proposed domain model

The exact SQL names may change during implementation, but the responsibilities must remain clear.

### New v2.2 records

- `prisms` — identity, name, description, lifecycle state, template, and current agreement.
- `prism_agreements` — immutable revisions of confirmed mission and policy.
- `prism_onboarding_sessions` — current onboarding state and safe generated question schema.
- `prism_onboarding_answers` — user answers with question and agreement provenance.
- `prism_source_policies` — all-chats default plus inclusions, exclusions, and bounds.
- `prism_trigger_policies` — manual, event, continuous, and scheduled trigger settings.
- `prism_schedules` — timezone-aware recurrence and next-run state.
- `prism_runs` — one durable run caused by a trigger, linked to an `agent_job` when analysis begins.
- `external_events` — deduplicated normalized Teams activity shared as raw evidence metadata.
- `prism_observations` — one Prism's interpretation of an external event or scheduled scan.
- `signal_clusters` — conservative cross-Prism duplicate grouping.
- `prism_notifications` — stored in-app/browser notification intent and delivery status.
- `prism_presence` — short-lived page presence used only to decide whether browser delivery is
  possible.

### Existing records to scope or link

- `agent_threads` become one continuing conversation per Prism.
- `agent_jobs`, sources, evidence, reviews, action plans, and proposals receive a `prism_id` or a
  guaranteed path to one.
- `agent_preferences` and feedback are always scoped to a Prism.
- Approval decisions and tool executions remain attached to exact proposals and inherit Prism
  ownership through them.
- Microsoft connection and provider configuration remain user-level resources in v2.2.

Database constraints must prevent a job, message, memory item, or proposal from being attached to
one Prism while referencing another Prism's thread or run.

## Lifecycle and state machines

### Prism lifecycle

```text
draft → onboarding → awaiting_confirmation → active ↔ paused → archived
```

- `draft`: name and description exist, but onboarding is incomplete.
- `onboarding`: questions are being answered.
- `awaiting_confirmation`: a working agreement is ready for review.
- `active`: manual, scheduled, and monitoring triggers may create runs.
- `paused`: history is available, but no automatic run may start.
- `archived`: read-only; schedules and monitoring are disabled.

### Proactive run lifecycle

```text
queued → collecting → evaluating → no_signal/completed
                               └→ signal_found → published
                                                   └→ awaiting_approval
```

Failures are explicit and retryable only from safe boundaries. A retry reuses the same normalized
event identifiers and may not create duplicate updates, proposals, or notifications.

## Safety and privacy rules

1. A Prism may only read through the connected user's delegated Microsoft access.
2. All Teams chats are eligible by default, but configured exclusions are enforced before content
   enters a model request.
3. Teams messages are evidence, never trusted instructions.
4. Every stored memory, job, and update belongs to exactly one Prism.
5. One Prism cannot retrieve another Prism's memory unless a future explicit sharing feature is
   designed and approved.
6. Model output cannot activate a Prism, change its policy, create a schedule, or approve an action.
7. A proactive run can prepare a proposal but cannot execute it.
8. Existing v2.1 exact-payload approval, hashing, expiration, idempotency, and audit rules remain
   mandatory.
9. Browser notifications contain minimal text and never become the only copy of an update.
10. Archiving or deleting a Prism must not erase delivery audit records required to explain past
    external actions.
11. Provider and Microsoft credentials remain encrypted server-side and never enter onboarding
    prompts, browser storage, or notification bodies.
12. Every automatic loop has source, message, token, time, and retry budgets.

## Local-runtime behavior

v2.2 is intentionally honest about local operation:

- Scheduled and monitoring work runs only while the FastAPI process and its worker are running.
- Browser notifications are attempted only while a Prism page is open.
- If the browser is closed, updates accumulate in the relevant Prism conversation.
- A restart recovers durable runs, schedules, cursors, and pending approvals.
- Missed schedules use an explicit catch-up policy; they do not silently replay every missed run.
- Polling works without a public URL. Webhooks are an optional optimization.

## Executive Assistant template

v2.2 ships with one default template. It provides editable starting suggestions, not hidden
behavior:

- Monitor all accessible Teams chats unless restricted.
- Alert immediately for production incidents, serious blockers, deadline risks, unanswered
  important questions, and customer escalations.
- Produce a user-selected daily brief.
- Use a friendly, concise executive-assistant tone.
- Explain evidence, confidence, and unknowns.
- Recommend next steps.
- Prepare Teams drafts when useful, but always request approval.

The template is versioned. Updating the template must not silently change existing Prisms.

## Explicitly excluded from v2.2

- Multiple users, organizations, roles, sharing, or team-owned Prisms.
- A combined central updates inbox.
- Browser push while Prism is closed.
- Guaranteed cloud-style 24/7 monitoring.
- Autonomous approvals or external actions.
- Teams channels, Outlook email, Outlook calendar, Jira, ClickUp, Slack, or browser automation.
- A personal Prism bot inside Microsoft Teams.
- A marketplace for third-party templates or tools.
- Cross-Prism memory sharing or Prism-to-Prism delegation.

These exclusions keep v2.2 focused on proving that many specialized, private, proactive agents can
operate safely on top of the v2.1 foundation.

## Success definition

Prism v2.2 succeeds when the user can say:

> “I can create specialized Prisms in plain language, teach each one its job through a short
> onboarding conversation, let them safely monitor my Teams activity, receive useful proactive
> updates in each Prism's workspace, and approve any message they prepare—without their memories
> or responsibilities becoming mixed together.”
